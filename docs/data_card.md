# Data card: synthetic Indian Type 2 diabetes twin dataset

_Numbers generated by `uv run python -m data_gen.describe` from dataset v0.1._

## Summary
- **What:** 400 synthetic adults with Type 2 diabetes, each with a static EHR record and 14 days of CGM (5-min), wrist-wearable heart rate, steps, sleep stages and HRV, and a partial meal log.
- **Why:** to build and evaluate a digital-twin prototype (2-hour glucose-spike prediction and what-if simulation) without any real patient data (DPDP Act / HIPAA).
- **Contains no real patient data.** Every record is simulated. Real open datasets were used only to calibrate a few population-level settings and to validate realism (see `docs/validation_report.md`).
- **No genetic data** of any kind.

## How it was made
1. Cohort layer: correlated sampling (Gaussian copula) of labs and anthropometrics with Indian-clinic-style marginals, then comorbidities, allergies, diet, habits and medications by rule (`data_gen/cohort.py`).
2. Latent physiology: hidden insulin sensitivity, beta-cell function and hepatic output from EHR fields plus unexplained variation; beta anchored so mean glucose matches the HbA1c (`data_gen/physiology.py`, decisions D-006, D-007).
3. Behaviour: Indian meals (47 plates), timing, portions, eating out, walking, sleep architecture, stress and illness days, heart rate and HRV (`data_gen/behaviour.py`).
4. Glucose-insulin ODE simulation at 1-minute steps with meals, drugs (as actually taken) and exercise.
5. Sensor layer: CGM lag, calibration error and drift, noise, warm-up, dropouts, compression lows; wearable gaps and artefacts (`data_gen/sensors.py`).

## Validation (summary)

- Real-vs-synthetic discriminator (12-h windows, level-matched): accuracy 0.88, AUC 0.95. Unmatched main cohort: accuracy 0.92.
- Full tables (level-matched dynamics, post-meal curves, TSTR) in `docs/validation_report.md`.
- TSTR on the Shanghai held-out half (CGM history only): 60-min MAE 18.7 mg/dL trained on synthetic vs 19.9 persistence and 17.5 trained on real; spike PR-AUC 0.34 vs 0.28 and 0.37.

## Intended use
- Developing and demonstrating the twin prototype, teaching, method comparison.
- **Not** for clinical decisions, prevalence estimates, or claims about real Indian patients.

## Known limitations
- **Circularity.** The simulator encodes our assumptions about how EHR fields, behaviour and physiology relate. A model trained on this data can partly rediscover those rules. Mitigations: hidden per-patient variation the record does not explain (insulin sensitivity and beta-cell noise, drug response, gastric emptying, true vs reported adherence and dinner time, meal-level absorption jitter, day-level stress, sleep and illness), sensor noise, and validation against real CGM. Performance on this data is an upper bound for performance on real patients, and explanation checks validate against the simulated world only.
- **Glucose level.** Anchored to HbA1c via the ADAG relation (outpatients); the two real datasets sit lower because they record monitored, treated periods (decision D-010).
- **Population values are assumptions.** Indian-specific marginals, prevalences, meal macros and drug effects are informed guesses awaiting checks (`docs/parameters_to_verify.md`, `docs/clinical_rules_to_review.md`).
- **Treatment scope.** Oral agents and basal insulin only; no prandial/premixed insulin, GLP-1 agonists, pioglitazone or acarbose.
- **Wearables** are plausible by construction but not validated against real wearable data (the real CGM datasets used have no sleep/HRV).
- **Meals** are complete 'plates' at standard portions with rough macros; no mixed-meal glycaemic-index interactions beyond fat, protein and fibre slowing.
